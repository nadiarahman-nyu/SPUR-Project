H&M, AI & sustainability

These notes summarize what the available sources say about H&M Group’s demand-planning work and its possible environmental relevance. The evidence supports a potential waste-reduction mechanism, but does not establish that AI caused company-wide emissions reductions.

Research caution: “AI for sustainability” can overstate the evidence. The H&M/IDEO case describes an algorithm and planning workflow; its pilot figures are inventory and sales outcomes, not a measured reduction in textile waste, carbon emissions, water use, or labor impacts.
What the case describes

Demand planning and “Cruise Control”

IDEO’s case study says IDEO and H&M Group Design Studio mapped the supply chain and created an algorithm to improve demand estimates and shorten the interval between sales and production. A shared planning tool, described as “Cruise Control,” gives teams a forecast to support more precise ordering.

The stated business problem was having too much stock of some items while customers could not find other basics. The workflow is intended to make decisions more responsive to market demand.

Reported pilot result

Promising, but limited evidence

22% less stock34% higher sales

IDEO attributes these figures to a pilot program. They should not be read as chain-wide results or as direct measurements of garments kept out of landfill. The public case page does not provide a full evaluation method, comparison period, or a quantified carbon/waste outcome.

Why it could matter

Preventing excess at the source

If improved estimates lead to fewer unnecessary units being made, they may avoid some of the material, energy, transport, and end-of-life impacts associated with those units. Preventing unnecessary production is different from producing excess and then relying on discounting, resale, donation, or recycling.

That benefit depends on real production and disposition data. Better forecasting alone is not proof of less production or environmental harm.

Company climate reporting

Keep emissions claims separate

H&M Group’s climate page reports progress across its value chain and points to factors including renewable electricity, energy efficiency, material efficiency, and recycled materials. Its current page cites 2025 report progress against a 2019 baseline.

Those company-wide figures are not attributed to the demand-planning algorithm. Do not treat them as evidence of AI-caused emissions reductions.

Open questions for further research

What would demonstrate a sustainability benefit?

  • Did the process reduce units ordered and produced, not only inventory held at a snapshot?
  • How many unsold garments were avoided, and what happened to any remaining surplus?
  • Are impacts measured with comparable product-level material and lifecycle emissions data?
  • Did the system protect availability without speeding up work through unsafe overtime, unrealistic lead times, or pressure on suppliers?
  • Do outcomes persist across seasons, brands, regions, and different product categories?
